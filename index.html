Mountain mornings. Golf by noon. Neighbors by evening.

Kenmure is 715 homes and 120 homesites on the Blue Ridge escarpment in Flat Rock, five miles south of Hendersonville. Golf and a private club at its center. Roads, gate, trash and snow handled by an association your neighbors run themselves, and have since 2002.

Why Kenmure

The mountains, without giving up the town.

Kenmure sits where the Blue Ridge begins. It feels tucked away, and it isn’t. Hendersonville’s Main Street is five miles up the road. Groceries, doctors and good restaurants are minutes from the gate, and I-26 puts Asheville and Greenville within an easy drive.

You get quiet roads, long views and cool evenings. You keep the conveniences that make a place easy to live in for the long run. That’s the trade most mountain living asks you to make, and here you don’t have to.

Photo: fall color along a Kenmure road, or a long view from the escarpment

Errands in minutes

Shopping, dining and medical care in Hendersonville are a short drive away. Three commercial airports sit within 100 miles, so family visits and travel stay simple.

One fee covers the basics

The annual fee is $1,783 for 2026. It pays for the private roads, the staffed gate, trash collection and snow removal, with reserves set aside for what comes next. Club membership is your choice, billed separately.

A place that’s lived in

Most households live here all year. Neighbors know each other, the calendar stays full, and the community is as awake in January as it is in July.

Seasons you can feel

Spring blooms, summer evenings that cool off, October color on every road. In winter the Association plows all 22 miles, so getting out is never the hard part.

Life here

Mountain quiet, with a town five minutes away.

People come to Kenmure for the same reasons they come to Flat Rock: the elevation, the seasons, and a pace that has nothing to prove. They stay because the place is easy to live in, whether that means twelve months a year or the ones that matter most to you.

The setting

Flat Rock, Henderson County

Kenmure sits on Greenville Highway (NC 225), five miles south of downtown Hendersonville and its Main Street. Flat Rock itself is home to the Flat Rock Playhouse and the Carl Sandburg Home. Asheville's airport is 20 miles away, Greenville-Spartanburg 38, and Charlotte 97, which makes visiting family and leaving for a season both simple.

The club

Kenmure Country Club

At the center of the community is a privately owned club managed by Pinnacle Golf, with golf, dining, tennis, pickleball, indoor and outdoor pools, and fitness. Membership is optional, never a condition of ownership, and club dues are entirely separate from the association's annual fees. Many residents join. Some never do. Both are at home here.

The neighbors

Full-time by a wide margin

About three in four households live here year-round, so the community is awake in February as well as October. Kenmure Newcomers & Neighbors introduces you to new and longtime residents through small activity groups that meet in each other's homes: games, dining get-togethers, book club, wine tasting and more, in three sign-up rotations a year. Any Kenmure resident or renter may join; after the first year, staying in KNN requires a Kenmure Country Club membership. For second-home owners, a staffed gate and roads plowed before you arrive mean the house is ready when you are.

Come see it for yourself.

Every community has a personality, and the only reliable way to judge ours is to spend time in it. Before you buy, walk the roads. Ask residents how the Board responds when something breaks. Ask what has changed in the last five years, and what is coming.

Your agent can arrange a showing; the rest is up to you and an afternoon.

Governance

No management company. Your neighbors, elected.

Buying into a community association means going into business with the people next door. Here is who they are and how the business is run.

The Kenmure Property Owners Association (KPOA) was formed in 2002 under North Carolina's Planned Community Act (Chapter 47F). Every property owner becomes a member at closing, and membership ends only when you sell all real estate you own in Kenmure. The governing documents are the Declaration, the Bylaws and the Articles of Incorporation.

A seven-member Board of Directors runs the Association. Owners elect directors to three-year terms, two or three seats each year, and the Board chooses its own President and Vice President. Volunteer committees handle community standards compliance, road maintenance, security, emergency preparedness and other functions. KPOA is the largest homeowners association in North Carolina run entirely by volunteers.

What that means in practice: no management company taking a percentage, and no layer of paid staff between owners and the people making decisions. The tradeoff is honest too. The Association depends on residents willing to serve, and it has found them for more than two decades.

If you're buying a condominium or cottage

Eleven condominium and cottage associations operate within Kenmure. Each has its own governing documents, board, budget and reserves. They must comply with KPOA's documents, but their finances are separate, and this page describes only KPOA's.

Lenders now look harder at condominium association finances than at community associations like KPOA, and a condominium association's reserve levels can affect a mortgage.

Mission

Keep Kenmure a premier place to live.

Enhance the Kenmure residential community under the leadership of an elected Board of Directors. The Association is organized to fund and provide essential services, and is guided by respect among members, high regard for its volunteers, and interest in the ongoing development of Kenmure as a premier place to live.

Objectives
  1. Provide essential servicesRoad maintenance, gated security, trash removal, snow removal, and others as appropriate.
  2. Protect property valuesArchitectural review, beautification, and the standards that keep the community looking like itself.
  3. Manage the common elementsThe roads, the entry gates, and Rail Pen Gap Park.
  4. Support community organizationsAn umbrella for the Welcoming Committee, Emergency Management, Kenmure Newcomers & Neighbors and others.
  5. Fund the future before it arrivesReserves sized so that predictable repaving and unpredictable storms are paid for without special assessments.

Annual fees

One fee. Everyone pays the same.

Every home, condominium and cottage pays one flat annual fee, set each fall by the Board. Lot owners pay less. There are no tiers and no add-ons for services, and the fee did not increase for 2026. The governing documents call this an assessment; we call it what it is.

Reserves

Paid for before it breaks.

KPOA keeps separate reserves for road maintenance, storm-related expenses, and general and administrative costs. Storm reserves are sized on what storms have actually cost. Administrative reserves are sized on past spending and on planned community improvement initiatives.

Storm response is the line that has moved most. Over 21 years Kenmure spent an average of $67,451 a year on storms and roadside tree work. Over the last five years that average is $127,151. The Association keeps a dedicated storm reserve, sized on that history, for exactly this reason.

Planned work

Budgeted before it starts.

Major work in Kenmure is planned through the same fall budget cycle that sets the annual fee, not through surprise assessments.

Road work follows the four-year engineering study, which tells the Board what the roads need and what it will cost to keep them in condition. Storm reserves are sized on the storms Kenmure has actually weathered. Community improvement initiatives the Board intends to pursue are funded through the administrative reserve, so the money is set aside before the work begins. Ask the Treasurer what is currently planned; the answer changes year to year, and the process does not.

Insurance

Steady, when the trend elsewhere is not.

Association insurance costs have risen sharply across the country in recent years. KPOA's insurance premium has increased from $5,136 in 2004 to $14,587 in 2024, about 5% a year.

Documents and due diligence

Ask for anything. We'd rather you did.
